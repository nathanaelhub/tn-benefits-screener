# Staff guide: using the benefits screener with a client

One page for front-desk staff, volunteers and case managers.

## What it's for

A quick first answer to "could this family get food or health help?" for **SNAP (food
assistance), WIC and TennCare/CoverKids** in Tennessee, with the next step for each.
It takes about five minutes and a few questions.

## How to use it

1. Open Claude (with the screener installed) and describe the household in plain words,
   or let the client type. For example: *"Mom, 27, and a 2-year-old. She makes $450 a week.
   Rent $750, pays her own gas and electric."*
2. Answer Claude's follow-up questions. It asks only what it needs.
3. Read the result with the client. Each program gets one of these:

| It says | What it means | What to tell the client |
|---|---|---|
| **Likely** | Meets every income test the screener checks | "It looks good. Here's where to apply." |
| **Possibly** | Close to a limit, or depends on a rule that isn't settled | "It's worth applying. Here's why it's uncertain." |
| **Unlikely** | Over a limit | "It doesn't look likely, but you can still apply. Only the state decides." |
| **Caseworker needs to look** | A situation the screener doesn't handle (immigration status, college students, self-employment, disability or age rules) | "A caseworker can check this. Here's who to call." |

4. Give the client the links and phone numbers at the end. For anything else, **211** is
   free and local.

## Good to know

- SNAP amounts are **estimates**. The real amount depends on documents and the caseworker.
- Tennessee's SNAP income limit is in flux in 2026. If the screener says "possibly" because
  of it, encourage the client to apply.
- A pregnant woman just over WIC's limit can often still get WIC by getting TennCare first.
  The screener will say so.
- Immigration status doesn't affect WIC. U.S.-citizen children of non-citizen parents can
  often get help even when a parent can't. Point families with immigration concerns to a
  legal aid organization.

## Privacy

- Use "the client", "her son": **no names, Social Security numbers or addresses.** The
  screener doesn't need them.
- Don't paste case notes or documents into the chat.
- The screener doesn't save anything, but your Claude account keeps the conversation unless
  you delete it. Follow your organization's data policy.

## What it can't do

- Decide eligibility, or replace a caseworker, a 211 navigator, or legal advice.
- Handle people already receiving a program (other than noting it), or appeals.
- Help anyone leave information off an application. It will refuse, and so should we.

If a result looks wrong, note the household details (no names) and send them to the person
who maintains the screener (see `RUNBOOK.md`).
